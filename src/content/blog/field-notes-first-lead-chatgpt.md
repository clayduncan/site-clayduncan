*Two years, zero leads. Then I rebuilt the site for machines instead of people, and a veteran two states away found me by asking a chatbot.*

---

## The Takeaway

People are starting to ask instead of search. When someone asks AI a question, it answers, and it does not always send them to a list of links to pick from. If your information is not in a form that AI can read and repeat, you are not in that answer at all.

---

## What Happened

### The website that did nothing

A couple of years ago I paid somebody to build me a personal website.

In all that time it produced nothing. Not one lead. Not one call. I am not sure anyone ever looked at the data sitting on it, including me.

That is not a complaint about the guy who built it. It looked fine. It was just a brochure sitting on the internet, the way most of ours are.

### Why I rebuilt it

I had gotten convinced of something from watching how I personally use AI.

I do not search the way I used to. When I want to know something, I ask. And I get an answer, not ten blue links. Increasingly, so does everyone else.

That changes what it means to be findable. Traditional search optimization is about ranking on a page of results. This is different. It is about whether an AI, when someone asks it a question in your field, can find your information, understand it, trust it, and repeat it.

People call it answer engine optimization, or generative engine optimization. The name does not matter. What matters is the shift: you are not competing for a spot on a results page anymore. You are competing to be part of the answer.

I had nothing to lose. My site was producing zero.

### What I actually did

I ran deep research first, using AI, on what actually makes a site readable and quotable by these systems. Not guesswork, not a blog post about best practices. A real study of what separates sites that get cited from sites that do not, in my specific industry.

Then I scanned my old site's performance before I took it down, so I would have a real baseline instead of a feeling.

Then I had AI build the new one. Mine, my son Levi's, and a combined site for our team.

Then I waited, which is the part nobody tells you about. It takes roughly eight to twelve weeks for all the various crawlers to fully find and index a new site. There is a long stretch where you have done the work and nothing is happening and you have no idea if any of it worked.

### The phone call

About seven weeks in, somebody booked time on my calendar.

I assumed it was someone I knew. It usually is.

It was not. It was a veteran, retiring, moving to Huntsville from another city, with questions about a VA loan.

We talked. Good conversation. And at the end I asked him the question I always ask, mostly out of habit.

Where did you find me?

ChatGPT.

My jaw hit the floor. Two years of a website that had never produced anything, and the first lead it ever gave me did not come from a search engine at all. It came from someone having a conversation with an AI about moving to Alabama, and my information being good enough to end up in that answer.

---

## What Changed After That

The website was the beginning of it. What it turned into is the part I did not expect.

**This blog is run by me and AI, and nobody else.** I write the posts. I hand them over. They get published on a schedule. I usually write three or more at a time and hand them off together, and they go out once a week without me touching anything.

**I stopped paying anyone to make changes.** If I do not like something on my site, I say so and it gets fixed. No ticket, no waiting, no invoice for twenty minutes of work.

**I have scheduled tasks doing the maintenance.** A scheduled task is just a job that runs on its own at a set time. One of mine moves finished events off my calendar into a past-events section, so my site never shows a stale event again. I do not remember to do that. It just happens.

**I dropped the hosting and management bill entirely.** That was a few hundred dollars a year for a site that, again, had produced nothing.

### The dinner

I had dinner with my sister a while back. She is a real estate agent.

We sat down, I asked her a handful of questions about her business, and I set my AI agent to work while we ate.

By the time dinner was over, she had a website.

---

## Why It Happened

Two things have to be true at once here, and I want both of them on the record.

**The first is that the barrier really did drop.** Building a site used to mean hiring someone, waiting, paying, and then paying again every time you wanted a comma moved. That is no longer true, and the dinner story is not an exaggeration.

**The second is that it was not simple, and I refuse to pretend it was.** Getting the first one standing took real work. There are accounts to create, services to connect, hosting to configure, domains to point, permissions to sort out. A dozen things that have nothing to do with writing a website and everything to do with making one exist on the internet.

The dinner was fast because all of that was already built. My sister got a website in ninety minutes because I had spent weeks earlier making ninety minutes possible.

That is the honest version. The work moved. It did not disappear.

## What This Means for Your Business

**Ask AI about yourself right now.** Open ChatGPT and ask it to recommend a real estate agent in your city for whatever you specialize in. See what comes back. If it is not you, that is your answer about where you stand.

**Then ask it what it knows about you specifically.** By name. What comes back tells you what is actually out there in a form AI can read, which is often nothing like what you assume is out there.

**Write for the question, not for the keyword.** People do not ask AI "Huntsville realtor." They ask "what should I know about buying a house in Huntsville with a VA loan." Pages that answer real questions in plain language get used. Pages stuffed with keywords do not.

**Your specialty is the opening.** Broad claims do not get quoted. Specific expertise does. Relocation, VA, first-time buyers, a particular school district, a particular kind of property. That is what makes an AI reach for your page over the thousand generic ones.

---

## One Thing to Try This Week

Write one page that genuinely answers a question you get asked constantly.

Not a listing page. Not an about page. An actual answer to an actual question, the way you would explain it to a client sitting across from you.

Put it on your site.

That page is worth more to you than another round of keyword tuning, because it is the kind of thing that can end up in somebody's answer. And the person on the other end of that answer is already halfway to calling you, because they asked the question in the first place.
